## The Collective Threshold Model
### A Community-Held Approach to Reciprocal Economics

## 1. The Insight: From Individual Transaction to Collective Holding

Most pricing models—even progressive ones like sliding scales—still operate on an individual transaction basis. Each person calculates what they can or should pay. This creates subtle hierarchies: those who pay full price, those who receive "support," those who need "scholarships."

But what if we approached this differently? What if the community collectively held each offering into being?

The Collective Threshold Model works like this:

- Each offering has a transparent need—the resources required for it to happen
- People register their intention to participate
- Everyone contributes anonymously to a collective pool—whatever feels right to them
- When the threshold is met, the offering takes place
- No one knows who contributed what

The community—not individuals—brings the offering into existence. Like a forest that emerges from countless seeds, roots, and mycorrhizal connections, each event grows from the collective intention and support of those who wish it to exist.

## 2. Philosophical Foundation

### 2.1 Ubuntu in Economic Form

The African philosophy of Ubuntu teaches that "I am because we are." Individual identity emerges from community relationship. The Collective Threshold Model embodies this principle economically: the offering exists because we collectively make it so.

No single person "buys" or "receives" the offering. The community creates it together. Those who contribute more do so not out of charity toward others, but because the community's flourishing is their own flourishing.

### 2.2 Plant Wisdom: Distributed Support

In a forest, nutrients flow through mycorrhizal networks from trees with abundance to trees with need. No tree is stigmatized for receiving. No tree is praised for giving. The forest is this flowing. The collective threshold operates the same way—resources flow naturally from where they are to where they're needed, anonymously, without hierarchy.

### 2.3 Removing the Stigma of Need

Even the most progressive sliding scale still requires someone to identify themselves as "needing support." This creates invisible barriers. Some people won't participate rather than acknowledge need. Others feel shame in spaces where their reduced contribution is known.

Anonymity dissolves this entirely. When no one knows who contributed what, there is no stigma. Everyone participates as equals, held by the same community pot.

### 2.4 Trust as Foundation

This model requires—and cultivates—trust. We trust that people will contribute according to their genuine capacity and conscience. We trust that abundance will flow from those who have it. We trust that the community will hold the offering.

This trust is not naïve. It is the same trust that underlies all gift economies, all commons governance, all genuine community. And in practice, such trust-based systems tend to generate more resources than transactional ones, because people give generously when freed from calculation.

## 3. How It Works: The Mechanics

### 3.1 Setting the Threshold

Each offering has a transparent need—the minimum resources required for it to happen. This includes:

- Facilitator/instructor compensation
- Materials and supplies
- Space costs (if any)
- Administrative support
- Contribution to organizational sustainability

This threshold is published openly. Everyone can see exactly what resources are needed and why. Transparency builds trust.

### 3.2 Registration and Contribution (Separate Steps)

**Step 1: Express Intention**
People register their intention to participate. This is separate from contribution. Registration helps us understand demand and plan capacity.

**Step 2: Contribute to the Collective Pot**
Contributors give what feels right to them—anonymously. The contribution interface shows only the current total versus the threshold, not individual amounts. Contributions can be made in:

- Euros (via standard payment methods)
- UBECrc tokens (earned through environmental stewardship)
- Contribution hours (pre-arranged work that offsets monetary need)

### 3.3 The Threshold Moment

- **When the threshold is reached:** The offering is confirmed. All registered participants receive confirmation.
- **If threshold isn't reached by deadline:** Several options exist (see section 3.5).
- **If contributions exceed threshold:** Surplus flows into the Regeneration Fund (see section 4).

### 3.4 Anonymity in Practice

To maintain anonymity:

- Contribution amounts are never displayed individually
- Only the aggregate total is visible
- Zero-contribution participation is genuinely welcome (see 3.6)
- No receipts show contribution amounts unless specifically requested for tax purposes
- The system is designed so even administrators don't see individual amounts

### 3.5 When Threshold Isn't Met

If the collective pot doesn't reach the threshold by the deadline, several responses are possible:

- **Extend the deadline:** Give more time for contributions to accumulate
- **Draw from the Regeneration Fund:** Previous surpluses can cover shortfalls
- **Reduce scope:** Offer a shorter version that fits available resources
- **Postpone:** Move to a later date when community capacity may be greater
- **Community conversation:** Open dialogue about what's needed and possible

### 3.6 Participation Without Financial Contribution

Some community members may be in life circumstances where any financial contribution is impossible. This is explicitly welcomed. Their participation enriches the community; their presence is their contribution.

To protect dignity and maintain anonymity, zero-contribution participation requires no special process, no application, no explanation. People simply register and do not contribute financially. No one—including organizers—knows who has contributed what.

## 4. The Regeneration Fund

When collective contributions exceed an offering's threshold, the surplus doesn't disappear. It flows into the Regeneration Fund—a community resource that:

- Covers shortfalls when offerings don't reach threshold
- Seeds new offerings that might not otherwise happen
- Supports unexpected community needs
- Builds long-term resilience

The Fund operates like soil—receiving what falls, composting it into fertility, and making nutrients available where growth wants to happen. Its balance is publicly visible, another layer of transparency.

## 5. Integration with UBECrc Tokens

The Collective Threshold Model integrates seamlessly with the UBECrc token economy. Those who earn tokens through environmental monitoring and citizen science can contribute them to collective pots.

### 5.1 Token Contribution

Tokens convert at the same rate as their earning rate (approximately 7 UBECrc = €0.10). This means:

- A sensor host earning ~21,400 UBECrc monthly can contribute ~€300 worth to community offerings
- Regular phenomenological observers accumulate participation capacity through their stewardship
- Environmental care directly enables community learning

### 5.2 Closing the Loop

This creates a beautiful reciprocal cycle:

**Environmental Stewardship → Token Earning → Community Contribution → Learning → Deeper Stewardship**

The land gives, we observe and care, the observation generates tokens, tokens enable learning, learning deepens relationship, relationship motivates more care. The economy becomes truly ecological.

## 6. A Practical Example

**Weekend Workshop: "Thinking Like a Plant"**

| Component | Amount |
|---|---|
| Facilitator compensation (2 days) | €400 |
| Materials and supplies | €80 |
| Shared meal ingredients | €120 |
| Organizational sustainability contribution | €100 |
| **THRESHOLD** | **€700** |

**Scenario: 12 people register interest**

If this were traditional pricing at €60/person, we'd need 12 full-paying participants. But in the Collective Threshold Model:

- Three participants contribute €150 each (they're established professionals)
- Four participants contribute €50 each (what feels right for their circumstances)
- Two participants contribute 500 UBECrc each (~€70 equivalent from their token earnings)
- Two participants contribute €20 each (students)
- One participant contributes nothing financially (in difficult circumstances)

**Total:** €450 + €200 + €140 token equivalent + €40 = **€830**

The threshold (€700) is exceeded. The workshop happens. The €130 surplus flows to the Regeneration Fund. And no one knows who contributed what. Everyone participates as equals.

## 7. Communication Approach

### 7.1 The Invitation

Language matters. Here's how we frame offerings:

*Example offering announcement:*

> "This workshop needs €700 to come into being. We invite you to contribute what feels right from your current place in life. Your contribution is anonymous—only the collective total is visible. When we reach the threshold together, the workshop will take place. Register your intention, then contribute to the collective pot as your heart and circumstances guide you."

### 7.2 Guiding Questions for Contributors

Rather than suggested amounts, we offer questions for reflection:

- "What does this offering mean to me?"
- "What is my current capacity to give?"
- "What would I want others to contribute if they were in my situation?"
- "How can I participate in the community's flourishing?"

### 7.3 Progress Visibility

The offering page shows:

- The threshold amount and what it covers (full transparency)
- Current collective total (but never individual contributions)
- Number of registered participants
- Days remaining until deadline
- A visual progress indicator

## 8. Technical Implementation

### 8.1 Platform Requirements

The system needs:

- Registration system (separate from contribution)
- Anonymous contribution processing (Euro + UBECrc)
- Real-time aggregate display (threshold vs. current total)
- Automated threshold notification
- Regeneration Fund tracking

### 8.2 Anonymity Architecture

Technical anonymity requires careful design:

- Contribution records store only: timestamp, amount, offering ID—no contributor ID
- Token contributions processed through privacy-preserving mechanism
- No correlation possible between registration and contribution
- Administrative access limited to aggregates only

## 9. Closing: The Offering as Commons

In the Collective Threshold Model, each offering becomes a commons—a shared resource created and sustained by community. No one owns the workshop, the course, the event. We all hold it into being together.

This transforms the fundamental relationship between participants and offerings. We are not consumers purchasing a product. We are community members co-creating the conditions for collective learning and growth.

Like a plant that doesn't ask "what did each ray of sunlight contribute?" but simply photosynthesizes the collective light into life, our offerings grow from the collective contribution without tracking individual inputs.

*"We cultivate the conditions for people to think like a plant."*

And in this economic model, we practice what we teach: distributed intelligence, network thinking, generous giving, patient accumulation, and trust in the collective capacity of the community to bring forth what wants to emerge.
