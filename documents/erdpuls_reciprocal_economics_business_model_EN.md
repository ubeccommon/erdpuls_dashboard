## 1. Philosophical Foundation: Beyond Extraction

This business model emerges from a fundamental question: How do we create economic exchange that gives more than it takes?

Like a plant that produces oxygen, food, beauty, and shelter while simply being itself, Erdpuls operates on a principle of generative abundance rather than extractive scarcity. Our economic model does not exist alongside our educational mission—it is our educational mission made visible in exchange.

### 1.1 The Three Pillars of Our Economic Philosophy

**RECIPROCITY (not charity):** Every exchange creates value flowing in both directions. We do not give gifts—we participate in genuine exchange where all parties contribute and receive.

**INTERDEPENDENCE (not independence):** Individual flourishing depends on community wellbeing. Our pricing reflects that those with more capacity support those with less, not from charity but from recognition that we all benefit when everyone can participate.

**ROOTEDNESS (not extraction):** Like a plant committed to place, we invest in long-term relationships rather than one-time transactions. Value accumulates through sustained engagement, trust builds over seasons and years.

### 1.2 Rejecting the Illusion of Choice

Conventional pricing presents a false binary: either services are priced for those who can afford them (excluding many), or they are given free (creating dependency and unsustainability). This binary thinking—Affordability OR Sustainability—mirrors the same false choice that separates Technology from Nature, Facts from Wisdom, Progress from Preservation.

Our reciprocal model transcends this binary. Participation is not a transaction to be completed but a relationship to be cultivated. Value is not extracted but exchanged. Everyone contributes according to their capacity; everyone receives according to their need.

## 2. The Four Pathways of Participation

Every Erdpuls offering can be accessed through four distinct but interconnected pathways. These are not "tiers" implying hierarchy—they are pathways implying different routes to the same destination.

| Pathway | How It Works | Philosophical Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Full Rate | Pay the listed price (covers operational costs + supports others) | Plant generosity: giving more than taking |
| Supported Rate | Pay a reduced rate (50–80% of full rate) | Ubuntu: individual wellbeing depends on community |
| Contribution | Offset costs through labor, skills, or service | Reciprocity: value flows in multiple directions |
| Token Exchange | Use earned UBECrc tokens for participation | Environmental stewardship recognized as value |

### 2.1 Full Rate (Sustaining Contribution)

Those who pay the full rate are not simply "buying a service." They are making a sustaining contribution that covers operational costs while creating capacity for others to participate. This is plant generosity in economic form—contributing to the abundance that sustains the whole community.

*Who chooses this pathway:* Those with stable financial resources who wish to support the mission through financial contribution. Organizations and institutions budgeting for professional development.

### 2.2 Supported Rate (Community-Held Participation)

The supported rate acknowledges economic reality without creating charity dynamics. Participants self-select based on genuine need—no proof required, no questions asked. This trust is essential: we treat all participants as adults capable of honest self-assessment.

*Who chooses this pathway:* Students, educators on limited budgets, families, individuals with fluctuating income, those from economically disadvantaged regions, anyone for whom full rate presents genuine hardship.

*Suggested self-assessment:* "Would paying the full rate require me to sacrifice something essential (food, housing, healthcare) or prevent me from meeting basic obligations? If yes, the supported rate is for you."

### 2.3 Contribution Pathway (Skills-Based Exchange)

This pathway recognizes that value comes in many forms. Time, skill, knowledge, labor—all are genuine contributions that support the community. Participants negotiate specific contributions that offset some or all of the financial cost.

Types of contribution accepted:

- **Garden work:** Planting, weeding, harvesting, composting, infrastructure maintenance
- **Technical skills:** Sensor maintenance, data processing, documentation, translation (DE/EN/PL/UK)
- **Knowledge sharing:** Leading workshops on your expertise, mentoring other participants
- **Administrative support:** Communication, scheduling, outreach, materials preparation
- **Traditional knowledge:** Sharing cultural wisdom, plant knowledge, ecological observation from life experience

### 2.4 Token Exchange (Earned Participation)

UBECrc (UBEC Reciprocity Credits) earned through environmental monitoring and citizen science contributions can be exchanged for participation in courses, events, and services. This closes the reciprocal loop: environmental stewardship generates value that enables further learning and engagement.

Token earning opportunities:

- Environmental observations submitted through the monitoring system (7.14 UBECrc per observation)
- Pattern recognition bonuses for identifying significant environmental changes
- Sensor host contributions (providing infrastructure for monitoring equipment)
- Phenomenological observation submissions (qualitative environmental documentation)

## 3. Pricing Framework

The following framework establishes ranges rather than fixed prices, recognizing that the appropriate rate depends on context, duration, and the specific situation of participants.

### 3.1 Workshops and Courses

| Offering Type | Full Rate | Supported | UBECrc |
|---|---|---|---|
| Half-day workshop (3–4 hrs) | €35–50 | €15–25 | 250–350 |
| Full-day workshop (6–8 hrs) | €60–85 | €30–45 | 450–600 |
| Weekend intensive (2 days) | €100–150 | €50–80 | 750–1100 |
| Multi-week course (4–6 sessions) | €120–200 | €60–100 | 900–1500 |
| Seasonal program (3 months) | €250–400 | €125–200 | 1800–3000 |

### 3.2 Special Populations

**Children and Youth (under 18):** Always free or deeply reduced. Young people's participation is an investment in future generations. Parent/guardian pays supported rate; child accompanies free.

**Educators:** Automatic supported rate. We recognize that teachers often pay from personal funds for professional development.

**Family Rate:** One full-rate adult + each additional family member at 50%. Families participating together strengthen intergenerational learning.

**Cross-Border and Cross-Region Participants:** Automatic supported rate recognizing economic differences between regions while celebrating cross-border collaboration.

### 3.3 Organizational and Group Rates

Schools, community organizations, and groups receive different treatment based on their relationship and context:

- **Partner Schools:** Schools in ongoing partnership receive reduced rates and priority scheduling
- **Community Organizations:** Local community groups negotiate based on capacity and reciprocal potential
- **Corporate/Institutional:** Organizations with institutional budgets pay full rates, enabling subsidy of community participants
- **Research Partners:** Academic and research institutions negotiate data-sharing and collaboration agreements

## 4. Token Integration: The Reciprocal Loop

The UBECrc token system creates a closed loop of reciprocal value: environmental stewardship generates tokens that enable participation, participation deepens understanding that motivates further stewardship.

### 4.1 How Tokens Are Earned

| Activity | UBECrc Earned | Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| Sensor observation submission | 7.14 | Up to 96/day |
| Phenomenological observation (qualitative) | 15–50 | Per submission |
| Pattern recognition bonus | 50–200 | When applicable |
| Sensor hosting (monthly) | ~21,400 | Monthly |
| Garden work contribution hour | 50–75 | Per hour |

### 4.2 Token-to-Euro Exchange Rates

For participation purposes, tokens convert at approximately 7 UBECrc = €0.10 (equivalent to the earning rate). This means:

- A half-day workshop (€35 full rate) = 2,450 UBECrc
- One month of sensor hosting (~21,400 UBECrc) covers multiple workshop participations
- Regular phenomenological observers can accumulate significant participation credit

### 4.3 Hybrid Payment

Participants can combine pathways. For example: pay 50% supported rate in euros + 50% in UBECrc tokens, or contribute 2 hours of garden work + remaining balance in euros. This flexibility ensures no one is excluded due to single-dimension economic constraints.

## 5. Contribution Framework: Value Beyond Money

The contribution pathway requires clear structure to ensure fairness while maintaining flexibility. This framework establishes exchange rates and expectations.

### 5.1 Contribution Categories and Value

| Contribution Type | Examples | Value/Hour |
|---|---|---|
| Garden Labor | Weeding, planting, harvesting, composting, watering | €10–12 equivalent |
| Skilled Labor | Carpentry, electrical, sensor installation, equipment repair | €15–25 equivalent |
| Knowledge Sharing | Leading a session, mentoring, traditional knowledge transmission | €20–35 equivalent |
| Translation/Writing | DE/EN/PL/UK translation, documentation, content creation | €15–30 equivalent |
| Technical Support | Data processing, sensor calibration, web development | €20–40 equivalent |
| Administrative | Communication, scheduling, outreach, event support | €10–15 equivalent |

### 5.2 Negotiation Principles

Contribution arrangements are negotiated in advance, with clear expectations on both sides:

- **Initial conversation:** Participant expresses interest in contribution pathway and discusses available skills/time
- **Agreement:** Specific contribution scope, timeline, and equivalent value agreed in writing
- **Completion:** Contribution completed as agreed; participation access granted
- **Flexibility:** Adjustments made if circumstances change, maintaining reciprocal relationship

## 6. Implementation Guidelines

### 6.1 Registration Process

All registrations follow the same simple process, regardless of pathway:

- Register online or in person, selecting preferred pathway
- No justification required for supported rate—self-selection based on self-assessment
- Contribution and token pathways arranged through brief conversation
- Confirmation sent with any preparation materials

### 6.2 Communication Language

How we describe pricing matters. We avoid charity language that creates hierarchy and dependency:

| Avoid | Use Instead |
|---|---|
| "Scholarship" or "Financial aid" | "Supported rate" or "Community rate" |
| "Discounted" or "Reduced" | "Different pathways to participation" |
| "Charity" or "Donation" | "Reciprocal exchange" or "Contribution" |
| "Those who can afford it" | "Those choosing to support others" |
| "Pay what you can" | "Choose the pathway that fits your situation" |

### 6.3 Financial Sustainability

The model is designed for sustainability, not subsidy dependence:

- **Target ratio:** 40% full rate, 40% supported rate, 20% contribution/token
- **Full rates set to cover:** Operational costs + 25% for community subsidy pool
- **Contribution work creates:** Genuine value that offsets costs (not free labor)
- **Token exchange reflects:** Real environmental stewardship value, not subsidy

## 7. Closing: Economics as Ecology

This business model is not separate from our educational mission—it is our educational mission made manifest in exchange. Every registration decision becomes an opportunity for participants to practice the same reciprocal thinking we cultivate in our programs.

When someone pays full rate, they practice plant generosity—giving more than taking, contributing to abundance that sustains the community. When someone uses the supported rate, they practice interdependence—accepting community support while contributing their presence and participation. When someone contributes skills, they practice network thinking—recognizing that value flows through multiple channels. When someone uses earned tokens, they close the reciprocal loop—environmental stewardship becoming participation becoming deeper understanding becoming renewed stewardship.

*"We cultivate the conditions for people to think like a plant."*

This economic model cultivates those conditions. It does not merely enable access to our programs—it enacts the very principles those programs teach. Head, hands, and heart united not only in learning but in the economics that makes learning possible.
